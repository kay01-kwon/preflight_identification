\section{The question}

The delivered CoM offset is read from the static tip-over balance.
If that balance is written \emph{without} ground effect while the
vehicle actually tips \emph{with} it, the identified offset differs
from the true one. This note derives the difference in closed form,
separates it into its two channels, and evaluates both on the
campaign. The punchline: per tip direction the error is
millimetres, but the deliverable's direction pairing cancels the
dominant channel identically, leaving at most $0.67$ mm --- a
$4.31\%$ multiplicative scale factor plus a small geometric
asymmetry.

\section{The two balances}

Write the onset balance per tip direction $s = \pm 1$ in the
matched-thrust convention (companion document), with $l_s$ the
direction's pivot arm, $f_s$ the collective thrust at onset, and
$\Soff = W \times \text{(signed offset)}$:
\begin{equation}
  (1+\alpha_M)\,M_s \;+\; s\,\alpha_f f_s l_s
  \;=\; s\,(W - f_s)\,l_s \;+\; \Soff .
  \tag{1}
\end{equation}
The no-GE model reads the offset from the same data by solving
\begin{equation}
  \Sread{}_{,s} \;=\; M_s \;-\; s\,(W - f_s)\,l_s .
  \tag{2}
\end{equation}
Substituting (1) into (2) gives the per-direction reading error in
one line:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\Sread{}_{,s} \;=\; \Soff
  \;-\; \alpha_M M_s \;-\; s\,\alpha_f f_s l_s \;}
  \tag{3}
\end{equation}

\section{One direction alone: millimetres}

Both channels of (3) are large per direction: $\alpha_M M_s \approx
0.043 \times 1$--$1.8$ N\,m $\approx 40$--$80$ mN\,m, and
$\alpha_f f_s l_s \approx 0.043 \times 21 \times 0.11$--$0.14
\approx 100$--$130$ mN\,m. Divided by $W \approx 31$ N, a
single-direction offset reading is off by
\begin{equation*}
  \text{median } 5.2 \text{ mm}, \qquad \text{max } 6.5 \text{ mm}
\end{equation*}
over the campaign --- comparable to the offsets being identified.
This is the quantitative reason the deliverable never uses one
direction alone.

\section{The direction pair: what cancels, what survives}

The deliverable averages the two directions,
$\Sread = \tfrac12(\Sread{}_{,+} + \Sread{}_{,-})$. In (3) the
thrust-channel term carries the factor $s$, so its \emph{symmetric}
part --- the $\pm 5$ mm monster of the previous section --- cancels
identically, and
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\Soff - \Sread \;=\;
  \alpha_M\,\Mff \;+\; \frac{\alpha_f}{2}
  \big(f_+ l_+ - f_- l_-\big)\;}, \qquad
  \Mff = \tfrac12\,(M_+ + M_-),
  \tag{4}
\end{equation}
or in millimetres of offset, dividing by $W$. Two channels remain:

\emph{The moment channel} $\alpha_M \Mff / W$. Since
$\Mff \approx W \times \text{offset}$ up to the arm asymmetry, this
term is $\alpha_M$ \emph{of the offset itself}: the no-GE reading
under-estimates the offset magnitude by the factor $1/(1+\alpha_M)$
--- a multiplicative $4.31\%$ scale error that no pairing can
remove, because it multiplies the very quantity being delivered.

\emph{The thrust channel} $\tfrac{\alpha_f}{2}(f_+l_+ - f_-l_-)/W$.
Only the direction \emph{asymmetry} of $f l$ survives the pair; with
symmetric thrust and arms it vanishes exactly.

\section{Why the correction is reported, not applied}

(4) says the true offset is the read one \emph{plus} up to
$0.67$ mm; the honest question is whether adding it improves the
agreement with the load-cell truth. It does not
(\texttt{analysis/ge\_offset\_effect.py}): the corrected offsets sit
slightly \emph{farther} from truth, by an amount inside the scatter.
The reason is a degeneracy the static data cannot break --- a
common shift of the contact resultant by a few millimetres produces
the same signature, and the two mechanisms cannot be separated
without a second ground clearance. The correction is therefore
reported as a \emph{bound on the channel}: the delivered offset is
insensitive to the static ground effect at the $\le 0.7$ mm level,
dominated by the multiplicative $4.31\%$ of the moment channel.
