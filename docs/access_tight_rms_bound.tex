\section{The measurement and the fit}

The record in the window and the fitted model are
\begin{align}
  y_i &= C_1\big(\cosh(C_2\tau_i)-1\big) + e_\omega(\tau_i) + b + n_i,
  \tag{1}\\
  \hat\omega_i &= C_1^*\big(\cosh(C_2^*(\tau_i-\dtc^*))-1\big) + C,
  \tag{2}
\end{align}
and on the pre-onset segment the baseline matches the bias,
\begin{equation}
  C \approx b.
  \tag{3}
\end{equation}

\section{The residual, and what the shift absorbs}

Subtracting (2) from (1) with (3),
\begin{equation}
  r_i \;=\; y_i - \hat\omega_i
  \;=\; \underbrace{C_1\big(\cosh(C_2\tau_i)-1\big)
        + e_\omega(\tau_i)}_{\text{to be absorbed}}
  \;-\; C_1^*\big(\cosh(C_2^*(\tau_i-\dtc^*))-1\big) \;+\; n_i .
  \tag{4}
\end{equation}
The question is how much of the braced part a member of the family
can match. Why a $\sinh$ term, of all shapes? Two facts meet. First,
$e_\omega$ \emph{grows} like $\sinh$: its envelope (90) is
$E(\tau) \propto \sinh(C_2\tau)$, because the deviation rides the
instability's own growing mode --- so peeling off a $\sinh$ multiple
targets the dominant part of the error. Second, $\sinh$ is exactly
what the family can absorb: it is the tangent direction of the onset
shift, $\partial_{\dtc}\,C_1(\cosh(C_2(\tau-\dtc))-1)\big|_{0} =
-C_1C_2\sinh(C_2\tau)$, so adding $\beta\sinh$ lands inside the
family (made exact in (6)--(7)). Had the envelope been, say,
$\tau^2$-shaped, subtracting $\beta\tau^2$ would target the error
just as well and help not at all --- the family could not represent
it. The coincidence of the two --- the error's dominant shape
\emph{is} the direction the fit can absorb --- is the entire reason
the measured residuals sit flat. Add and subtract that one $\sinh$
term --- this \emph{defines} $\de$, and the equality holds at every
$\tau$, both window ends included:
\begin{equation}
  C_1\big(\cosh(C_2\tau)-1\big) + e_\omega(\tau)
  \;=\;
  \underbrace{C_1\big(\cosh(C_2\tau)-1\big)
   + \beta\sinh(C_2\tau)}_{\hat h(\tau),\ \text{the witness}}
  \;+\;\de(\tau).
  \tag{5}
\end{equation}
The witness is a member of the family in disguise. With the addition
identity
\begin{equation}
  \cosh\big(C_2(\tau-\dtc)\big)
  = \cosh(C_2\dtc)\cosh(C_2\tau) - \sinh(C_2\dtc)\sinh(C_2\tau),
  \tag{6}
\end{equation}
matching the $\cosh$ and $\sinh$ coefficients
[$R\cosh(C_2\dtc) = C_1$, $-R\sinh(C_2\dtc) = \beta$] gives,
\emph{exactly},
\begin{equation}
  \hat h(\tau)
  = R\big(\cosh(C_2(\tau-\dtc))-1\big) + (R-C_1),
  \qquad
  R = \sqrt{C_1^2-\beta^2},
  \quad
  \tanh(C_2\dtc) = -\beta/C_1 ,
  \tag{7}
\end{equation}
a branch with amplitude $R$, onset $\dtc$, and a baseline offset
$R-C_1$ --- three numbers, all inside the family's freedoms in (2)
(the offset is absorbed by $C$: the witness's baseline slot carries
$b + (R-C_1)$, not $b$ alone, and the ``$\approx$'' of (3) is exactly
this second-order constant; what it costs, and on which segment it
lands, is settled in the remark closing the pre-onset section). On
the campaign
$\beta/C_1 \le 0.023$, so the amplitude and baseline adjustments are
second order ($|R/C_1-1| \le 0.03\%$); the shift carries the
first-order content. The remainder is
\begin{equation}
  \de(\tau) \;=\; e_\omega(\tau) - \beta\sinh(C_2\tau).
  \tag{8}
\end{equation}

\section{Choosing the shift: match the true curve at the window end}

One free number, $\dtc$ (equivalently $\beta$), and one choice: make
the shifted branch meet the true curve at $\tau_{\rm end}$. Setting
$\de(\tau_{\rm end}) = 0$ in (8),
\begin{equation}
  \beta \;=\; \frac{e_\omega(\tau_{\rm end})}{\sinh(C_2\tau_{\rm end})},
  \qquad
  \dtc \;=\; -\frac{1}{C_2}\,
  \operatorname{artanh}\!\Big(\frac{\beta}{C_1}\Big)
  \;\approx\; -\frac{e_\omega(\tau_{\rm end})}
  {C_1C_2\sinh(C_2\tau_{\rm end})} .
  \tag{9}
\end{equation}
Since $e_\omega > 0$ (the true curve rises faster than the nominal),
$\dtc < 0$: the branch starts \emph{earlier} than the true onset. On
the campaign $\beta/C_1 = 0.010$--$0.023$, i.e.\ $|\dtc| = 2$--$4$
ms.

\emph{Admissibility --- why $|\beta| < C_1$ is guaranteed.} (7)
needs $R$ real, i.e.\ $|\beta| < C_1$; otherwise the witness is not
a cosh branch at all. This is provable, not assumed: the envelope (90)
gives $|e_\omega(\tau_{\rm end})| \le
\rbar\sinh(C_2\tau_{\rm end})/(J_PC_2)$, so
$|\beta| \le \rbar/(J_PC_2)$, and dividing by $C_1 = \Mdot/Wz_{CoM}$
with the identity $J_PC_2^2 = Wz_{CoM}$,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{|\beta|}{C_1} \;\le\; \frac{\rbar\,C_2}{\Mdot}
  \;\triangleq\; u .
  \tag{9a}
\end{equation}
So $|\beta| < C_1$ holds whenever $\rbar < \Mdot/C_2$ ---
\emph{the ramp must sweep more moment in one e-folding time $1/C_2$
of the instability than the window-averaged remainder}. (An
\emph{e-folding} is the natural unit of an exponential process: the
interval over which $e^{C_2\tau}$ grows by a factor $e$, i.e.\
$1/C_2 \approx 0.2$ s here --- the base-$e$ analogue of a half-life.
$\xe  = C_2\tau_{\rm end}$ counts how many such units the window spans;
this document measures time in $1/C_2$ throughout, because in that
unit the same formulas give the same numbers for every vehicle and
ramp rate.) A protocol
that violated this would have its excitation comparable to the model
error it is meant to dominate, so any meaningful design satisfies it
with room: $u \le 0.59$ at the campaign's slowest ramp, $\le 0.66$
at the worst design-box corner of Sec.~9, and the realised
$\beta/C_1 = 0.010$--$0.023$ sits thirty times below the ceiling.
The same $u$ is the argument of the $\operatorname{artanh}(u)/u$
factor in (21): one dimensionless number controls both the witness's
admissibility and the exactness of the shift ceiling.

\emph{What if $u \ge 1$ --- ramps slower than $\rbar C_2$.} Three
things, in decreasing order of certainty. (i) Only the \emph{a priori}
guarantee is lost: (9a) is sufficient, not necessary, so the witness
may well still be admissible --- the realised $\beta/C_1$ sits some
thirty times below the envelope ceiling on the campaign --- but
nothing proves it before the experiment any more. (ii) If
$|\beta| \ge C_1$ did occur, the combination
$C_1\cosh(C_2\tau) + \beta\sinh(C_2\tau)$ is no longer a cosh branch:
it equals $\sqrt{\beta^2 - C_1^2}\,\sinh\!\big(C_2\tau + \varphi\big)$
(a pure exponential at $|\beta| = C_1$), and no real time shift of the
family (2) draws a sinh-type curve. Step (10) then has no admissible
competitor to hand to the minimiser, and the cap loses its proof ---
even though the Dirichlet bound (11)--(17) on
$e_\omega - \beta\sinh(C_2\tau)$ itself survives, since it never used
admissibility. Moving the matching point earlier does not rescue it:
the envelope of $e_\omega$ and $\sinh(C_2\tau)$ grow in the same
ratio, so the required $\beta$ ceiling is the same at every matching
point. (iii) Physically, $\Mdot < \rbar C_2$ says the moment swept in
one e-folding of the instability no longer dominates the model
remainder --- the identification concept itself, not just its
certificate, is degrading; the campaign's slowest ramp already runs at
$u = 0.59$, close to that boundary. Numerically, $u = 1$ is reached at
$\Mdot = \rbar C_2 = 0.059$ N\,m/s for the campaign vehicle and at
$\Mdot = 0.054$ N\,m/s at the worst design-box corner of Sec.~9
($m = 3.22$ kg, $l_p = 0.140$ m, $p_{\rm off} = -0.020$ m,
$z_{CoM} = 0.20$ m); a hypothetical $\Mdot = 0.03$ N\,m/s ramp at that
corner would have $u = 1.81$. So (9a) doubles as an analytical
\emph{lower} rate limit for the protocol,
$\Mdot > \rbar C_2 \approx 0.06$ N\,m/s --- about half the slowest
campaign rate --- complementing the allocator-saturation \emph{upper}
limit on $\Mdot$. In practice the logic runs forward, and this is the
intended reading: the guarantee fails \emph{only} there, and since
$\rbar$ and $C_2$ need nothing but the coarse design-box ranges known
before any experiment, one computes the box-worst
$\rbar C_2 = 0.054$ N\,m/s in closed form first and then schedules
the ramp rates above that floor with margin --- the campaign's
$\Mdot \ge 0.10$ N\,m/s is a factor $\approx 2$ above it, giving
$u \le 0.66$ everywhere in the box.

\emph{Could the family be composed so the problem never arises?}
Partly, and where it splits is instructive. Replace the shifted-cosh
family (2) by its linear span, fitting
$\hat\omega = A(\cosh C_2\tau - 1) + B\sinh C_2\tau + C$: the witness
$\omega_{\rm nom} + \beta\sinh(C_2\tau)$ is then a member for
\emph{every} $\beta$ (take $A = C_1$, $B = \beta$, $C = b$), so the
argmin step (10) holds unconditionally and admissibility disappears
from the residual cap altogether. The added column is no stranger:
$\sinh$ is exactly the tangent direction of the time shift
($\partial_{\dtc}$ of the branch at $\dtc = 0$ is $-C_1C_2\sinh$,
Appendix~A.2), so this is the linearised version of the same
estimator. The cost is one more degree of freedom in the noise term
(minor) and a near-collinear pair of columns --- $\cosh-1$ and
$\sinh$ agree to $O(e^{-2\xe})$ at the window end --- so the residual
stays clean while the individual coefficients $A, B$ get noisy: the
ridge of Sec.~4, resurfacing as conditioning. What \emph{cannot} be
designed away is the readout. Converting $(A,B)$ back into an onset
time needs $R = \sqrt{A^2 - B^2}$ real, i.e.\ $|B| < A$ --- the very
same condition, now appearing where it belongs. $u < 1$ is not an
artifact of the cosh parametrisation; it is the statement that the
data actually describes a delayed cosh onset rather than a sinh-type
curve, and no reparametrisation makes data interpretable that is not.
The practical split: the linear family buys an \emph{unconditional}
residual cap, and $u < 1$ then governs only whether the fitted curve
has a physical onset to read off.

Then, because the minimiser in (2) is at least as good as this one
particular shifted branch,
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  \sum_{i=1}^{N} r_i^2
  \;\le\; \sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(\de(\tau_i) + n_i\big)^2
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \rms(r) \;\le\; \rms(\de) + \rms(n) . \;}
  \tag{10}
\end{equation}
Everything now rests on one question: \textbf{how big is $\de$?}

\section{In exactly what sense the true curve is a time-shifted
nominal}

The ansatz of (5) deserves a precise statement, because ``the true
model is the nominal, time-shifted'' is true in one sense, exactly, and
false in another, and the gap between the two \emph{is} $\de$.

\emph{The local (osculating) sense --- exact at every instant.}
Splitting the manuscript's Duhamel kernel (89) with the addition
identity gives the deviation two running coefficients,
\begin{equation}
  e_\omega(\tau) = A(\tau)\cosh(C_2\tau) - B(\tau)\sinh(C_2\tau),
  \quad
  A = \frac1{J_P}\!\int_0^\tau\!\cosh(C_2 s)\rho\,ds,
  \quad
  B = \frac1{J_P}\!\int_0^\tau\!\sinh(C_2 s)\rho\,ds,
  \tag{10a}
\end{equation}
so the true structural curve is
$(C_1{+}A)\cosh(C_2\tau) - B\sinh(C_2\tau) - C_1$. Freezing the
coefficients at an instant $\tau$ and applying the shift identity (6)
backwards, the curve agrees there --- in value \emph{and} slope ---
with exactly one branch, the \emph{osculating branch}, whose
parameters are
\begin{equation}
  R(\tau) = \sqrt{\big(C_1{+}A(\tau)\big)^2 - B(\tau)^2},
  \qquad
  \tanh\!\big(C_2\,\delta t_{\rm loc}(\tau)\big)
  = \frac{B(\tau)}{C_1 + A(\tau)} .
  \tag{10b}
\end{equation}
At the onset $A = B = 0$, so $R = C_1$ and $\delta t_{\rm loc} = 0$:
\textbf{the true curve starts as the nominal, exactly.} As the window
progresses the osculating parameters drift monotonically (measured on
the exact tip-over: $\delta t_{\rm loc} = 0$ at the onset, still only
$2.5$/$0.4$/$0.1$ ms at mid-window for $\Mdot = 0.10$/$0.45$/$1.20$,
then $546$/$239$/$85$ ms at the window end, with
$R(\tau_{\rm end})/C_1 = 13.0$/$3.1$/$1.5$). The late blow-up is
structural: $e_\omega$'s tail grows like $e^{2C_2\tau}$, faster than
any branch, so the branch that hugs it must run away in amplitude and
shift together --- and those two parameters are ridge-degenerate,
wildly different $(R, \delta t)$ pairs drawing nearly the same curve
over the window. This is the same degeneracy that makes
$(C_1^*, C_2^*, \dtc^*)$ unidentifiable from the residual.

\emph{The window ($L^2$) sense --- the estimator's sense.} No single
branch can follow the drifting profile (10b); a fit must pick one
point of it. The least-squares stationarity
$\langle r, \sinh\rangle = 0$ weights the choice by $\sinh^2(C_2\tau)$,
which concentrates $63\%$ of its mass in the last e-folding
$1/(2C_2) \approx 90$--$140$ ms of the window ($86\%$ in the last
two), so the fitted single shift is essentially the end-matching one
of (9) --- and it is \emph{small}: $\beta/C_1 = 1.0$--$2.3\%$, i.e.\
$|\dtc| = 2$--$4$ ms on the campaign. In this sense the true curve
\emph{is} a slightly-shifted nominal: one branch, shifted by
milliseconds, matches it over the whole window up to exactly $\de$ ---
which vanishes at both ends and sags mid-window where the drifting
profile is farthest from its end value. That sag is what (11)--(17)
bound.

One sign remark connects the two senses. The osculating description
carries a \emph{larger} amplitude with a \emph{later} onset
($\delta t_{\rm loc} > 0$); the witness of (9) carries the nominal
amplitude with an \emph{earlier} onset ($\dtc < 0$). Along the ridge
these describe nearly the same curve --- larger-and-later
$\approx$ same-and-earlier --- and the deployed estimator, whose
amplitude is pinned to $K\Mdot$, realises the earlier-onset
representative: the sign observed on the campaign, and the sign whose
$M_{\rm crit}$ cost (21) charges.

\emph{Remark --- a perfect fit at all $N$ samples is impossible, and
that is not a defect of the fit.} Two reasons, one counting and one
dynamical. Counting: a branch has four free numbers
$(C_1^*, C_2^*, \dtc^*, C)$, so it can be made to pass through at most
four chosen samples; the other $N-4$ dimensions of the sample space
are out of its reach, and $e_\omega$ has components there. Dynamical,
and stronger: if a branch agreed with the true curve on any
subinterval, then $\de \equiv 0$ there, and (13) would force
$\dot\rho \equiv 0$ on that subinterval --- the true curve solves the
\emph{forced} equation, the branches solve the homogeneous one, and
they can coincide on an interval only where the forcing vanishes.
Exact agreement at $\tau_1, \dots, \tau_N$ is therefore not
``difficult''; for $\dot\rho \not\equiv 0$ it is unattainable in
principle.

This is why the section's claim is framed as it is. ``The true curve
is a time-shifted nominal'' cannot be asserted exactly; asserted with
the remainder, it \emph{cannot be wrong}: eq.\ (5) is an identity ---
$\de$ is defined as whatever the shifted branch misses --- and the
entire scientific content lives in one place, the bound (17) on that
remainder. The structure is that of Taylor's theorem: ``$f$ is
approximately a polynomial'' is a hope, ``$f$ equals the polynomial
plus a remainder obeying a stated bound'' is a theorem. Here the
remainder is $0.16$--$0.17^\circ$/s realised, capped by (17) at
$0.5$--$0.9^\circ$/s from pre-experiment constants, and located ---
zero at both ends, sagging mid-window --- so the imperfection of the
time-shift description is quantified in size, ceiling and shape.

The same statement has a clean linear-algebra reading: the model
family is a four-parameter manifold in the $N$-dimensional sample
space, the true record carries a component transverse to it that no
choice of parameters can reach, and $\de$ is exactly that transverse
coordinate --- introduced so that the description becomes an identity
rather than an approximation. The Appendix makes this picture precise
and connects it to the manuscript's projector formalism.

\section{How big is $\de$: two boundary zeros and one comparison}

\emph{Step 1 --- $\de$ vanishes at both ends.} At $\tau = 0$:
$e_\omega(0) = 0$ (the deviation starts at rest) and $\sinh(0) = 0$,
so $\de(0) = 0$. At $\tau = \tau_{\rm end}$: zero by the choice (9).
\begin{equation}
  \de(0) \;=\; \de(\tau_{\rm end}) \;=\; 0 .
  \tag{11}
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 2 --- the equation $\de$ obeys.} The deviation dynamics of
the manuscript are $J_P\ddot e_\varphi = Wz_{CoM}\,e_\varphi + \rho$
with $e_\omega = \dot e_\varphi$. Differentiating once and using
$Wz_{CoM} = J_PC_2^2$,
\begin{equation}
  \ddot e_\omega \;=\; C_2^2\,e_\omega + \dot\rho/J_P .
  \tag{12}
\end{equation}
$\sinh(C_2\tau)$ satisfies $(\sinh)'' = C_2^2\sinh$ --- it is a
solution of the same equation with zero forcing --- so subtracting
$\beta\sinh$ changes nothing on the right side:
\begin{equation}
  \ddot{\de} \;=\; C_2^2\,\de + \dot\rho/J_P ,
  \qquad \de(0)=\de(\tau_{\rm end})=0 .
  \tag{13}
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 3 --- comparison (this is the whole proof).} Let
$\bar f(\tau) \ge |\dot\rho(\tau)|$ and let $w$ solve the same problem
with forcing $-\bar f$:
\begin{equation}
  \ddot w = C_2^2 w - \bar f/J_P,
  \qquad w(0) = w(\tau_{\rm end}) = 0 .
  \tag{14}
\end{equation}
Claim: $|\de(\tau)| \le w(\tau)$. Proof in two lines: the difference
$u = w - \de$ satisfies $\ddot u - C_2^2 u = -(\bar f + \dot\rho)/J_P
\le 0$ with $u = 0$ at both ends --- note the $\le 0$ belongs to this
\emph{combination}, at every $\tau$, because $\bar f \ge |\dot\rho|$
makes $\bar f + \dot\rho \ge 0$. If $u$ had a negative interior
minimum, there $\ddot u \ge 0$ (the valley bottom bends upward:
$\dot u = 0$ there, and $u(\tau^\ast{+}h) = u(\tau^\ast) +
\tfrac12\ddot u(\tau^\ast)h^2 + o(h^2) \ge u(\tau^\ast)$ for both
signs of $h$ forces $\ddot u \ge 0$) and $-C_2^2u > 0$ (strictly,
because $u < 0$ there --- a minimum value $\ge 0$ would give
$-C_2^2u \le 0$ and no contradiction), so
$\ddot u - C_2^2u > 0$ --- contradicting the $\le 0$ that holds
everywhere. Hence no negative interior minimum exists; with $u = 0$
at both ends and $u$ continuous, $u \ge 0$, i.e.\
$\de \le w$; the same argument with $u = w + \de$ gives $\de \ge -w$.
$\square$

\emph{Step 4 --- solve (14) in closed form.} For constant
$\bar f = \sup|\dot\rho|$, substitution verifies
\begin{equation}
  w(\tau) \;=\; \frac{\sup|\dot\rho|}{J_PC_2^2}
  \left(1 - \frac{\cosh\!\big(C_2(\tau - \tfrac{\tau_{\rm end}}2)\big)}
  {\cosh(\xe/2)}\right)
  \;\le\; \frac{\sup|\dot\rho|}{Wz_{CoM}} .
  \tag{15}
\end{equation}
(Here $\xe  = C_2\tau_{\rm end}$: at $\tau = 0$ and $\tau = \tau_{\rm end}$
the numerator equals $\cosh(\xe/2)$, so the bracket vanishes at both ends;
it peaks at mid-window, where the bound is
$(1-\operatorname{sech}(\xe/2))\sup|\dot\rho|/Wz_{CoM}$.)
\textbf{No exponential appears.} The envelope (90) grows like
$\sinh(C_2\tau)$ because nothing stops the unstable mode of (12);
here the two boundary zeros of (11) --- one free, one bought with
$\dtc$ --- pin that mode down. This is what ``the fit absorbs the
growing part'' means as an equation.

\emph{Step 5 --- the forcing rate, from the same channels as
$\rbar$.} Differentiating the gravity remainder (84) in time gives
$\dot\rho_{\rm grav} = \big[W l_{\rm arm}\sin\varphi
+ Wz_{CoM}(\cos\varphi - 1)\big]\,\omega$, and the two bracket terms
have \emph{opposite signs}: the height term is negative and cancels
against the arm term instead of adding to it. The bracket stays
inside $[0,\ W l_{\rm arm}\sin\varphi]$ as long as
$l_{\rm arm} \ge z_{CoM}\tan(\varphi/2)$ --- held with a factor
$3.4$ over the whole design box ($0.090$ m against at most
$0.30\tan 5^\circ = 0.026$ m) --- so $Wz_{CoM}$ drops from the bound
\emph{by sign, not by approximation}. This is the same cancellation
$\rbar$ (91) already uses ($Wz_{CoM}(\sin\varphi-\varphi) \le 0$
there), and it is exactly what the manuscript's (85) says at
$\varphi^\ast = 0$: $G''(0) = W(l_p + p_{\rm off})$, the
$Wz_{CoM}\sin\varphi^\ast$ term vanishing. Bounding on the $10^\circ$
box, with $\omega_{\max} = K\Mdot\sinh \xe$:
\begin{equation}
  \sup|\dot\rho| \;\le\;
  \underbrace{W l_{\rm arm}\varphi_{\max}\,
  \omega_{\max}}_{\dot\rho_2,\ \text{decays like } e^{2C_2(s-\tau_{\rm end})}}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{|\beta_M|\big(\Mdot\varphi_{\max}
    + \Delta M_{\rm win}\,\omega_{\max}\big)}_{
    \dot\rho_1,\ \text{decays like } e^{C_2(s-\tau_{\rm end})}} .
  \tag{16}
\end{equation}
Every constant is available before the experiment: $W$, $z_{CoM}$ from
the scale, $J_P$ from CAD, $l_{\rm arm}$ from geometry,
$\varphi_{\max}$ the design box, $\beta_M$ from the GE model, $\Mdot$
and the window from the protocol.

\emph{What ``decays like'' means --- the bridge from (16) to (16a).}
Nothing in the window decreases with time: $\varphi$ and $\omega$
both grow. The decay is the same fact read \emph{backwards from the
window end} --- the claim under each brace of (16) is
\begin{equation*}
  |\dot\rho_{\rm channel}(s)| \;\le\;
  |\dot\rho_{\rm channel}(\tau_{\rm end})|\;
  e^{kC_2(s-\tau_{\rm end})},
  \qquad s \le \tau_{\rm end},\ \ k \in \{2, 1\},
\end{equation*}
where the exponential factor is $< 1$ because $s - \tau_{\rm end} <
0$: at earlier times the forcing sits below its end value by at least
that factor. This is the form the comparison problem of Step 6
consumes --- a peak at the end with a known fall-off behind it.
Divide both sides by $e^{kC_2 s}$ and the claim becomes
$|\dot\rho(s)|/e^{kC_2 s} \le |\dot\rho(\tau_{\rm end})|/
e^{kC_2\tau_{\rm end}}$ --- i.e.\ \emph{the ratio
$|\dot\rho(s)|/e^{kC_2 s}$ is nondecreasing in $s$}. That is why
(16a) is a statement about ratios. And because each channel is a
\emph{product} of trajectory factors,
$\dot\rho/e^{kC_2 s}$ factors as a product of single-factor ratios
($\varphi\omega/e^{2C_2 s} = [\varphi e^{-C_2 s}][\omega e^{-C_2
s}]$), a product of nonnegative nondecreasing functions is
nondecreasing, and the exponent $k$ in (16) is literally a
\emph{count of trajectory factors}: two in $\varphi\,\omega$
($k = 2$), one in each ground-effect term ($k = 1$).

\emph{Why anchor at the end rather than the onset.} Four reasons,
the last the deepest. A forward envelope $|\dot\rho(s)| \le
|\dot\rho(0)|e^{kC_2 s}$ has nothing to hang on: the forcing
\emph{vanishes} at onset ($\varphi = \omega = 0$), so its anchor
amplitude is zero; the one usable anchor is the peak --- the end
value, which any bound must pay anyway and which the channel sups
bound a priori. Second, the tightness lives in ``how little forcing
there is early'': the Dirichlet response has a boundary zero directly
under the forcing peak, and exploiting that cancellation requires the
envelope to encode the fall-off \emph{behind} the peak --- exactly
what the backward rate is, and exactly what a constant
(sup-only) envelope discards at a cost of $8$--$12\times$. Third,
only the end-anchored coordinate $u = C_2(s - \tau_{\rm end}) \in
[-\xe, 0]$ makes the comparison problem forget the window length: as
$\xe  \to \infty$ the end neighbourhood is fixed and only the onset
recedes, which is why the $\xe$-free constants $1/12$ and $1/(2e)$
exist at all; in an onset-anchored coordinate the peak would move
with $\xe$ and no such constants would emerge. Last, the instability
has one clock, $1/C_2$, and everything that matters happens in the
last few e-foldings before $\tau_{\rm end}$: \emph{measured from the
end, all windows look alike}. That statement is the coordinate
version of the rate-flatness of the residual --- the ramp rate only
moves the onset distance $\xe$, and the end neighbourhood forgets
$\xe$.

\emph{Step 5$'$ --- the decays marked in (16) are theorems, not
observations.} Along the nominal trajectory, write $a = C_2 s$. By
the bridge above, it suffices that each factor's $e^{C_2 s}$-ratio is
nondecreasing; the first ratio of (16a) certifies the factor
$\omega e^{-C_2 s}$, the second certifies $\varphi e^{-C_2 s}$, and
both facts are one line each:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\omega_{\rm nom}(s)}{C_1\,e^{C_2 s}}
  = \frac{\big(\cosh a - 1\big)}{e^{a}}
  = \frac{\big(1-e^{-a}\big)^2}{2}\ \nearrow,
  \qquad
  \frac{d}{da}\!\left[\frac{\sinh a - a}{e^{a}}\right]
  = e^{-a}\big(a - 1 + e^{-a}\big) \;\ge\; 0,
  \tag{16a}
\end{equation}
the first read off directly, the second because $e^{-t}\ge 1-t$; the
bracket in the second is $\varphi_{\rm nom}\,C_2 e^{-C_2 s}/C_1$, so
$\varphi_{\rm nom}e^{-C_2 s}$ is nondecreasing too. Now every channel
of $\dot\rho$ is a product of positive factors whose
$e^{C_2 s}$-ratios are nondecreasing, and such a product obeys its
decay exactly. Arm channel:
\[
  \sin\varphi\,\omega \;\le\; \varphi\,\omega
  = \big[\varphi e^{-C_2 s}\big]\big[\omega e^{-C_2 s}\big]e^{2C_2 s}
  \;\le\; \varphi(\tau_{\rm end})\,\omega(\tau_{\rm end})\,
  e^{2C_2(s-\tau_{\rm end})} .
\]
(The height term, had it been kept, is
$(1-\cos\varphi)\,\omega \le \tfrac12\varphi^2\omega$ --- \emph{three}
such factors, decaying as $e^{3C_2(s-\tau_{\rm end})}$; it is absent
from (16) by the sign cancellation of Step 5, so nothing needs
charging.) Ground effect: $\Mdot\varphi$ carries one factor
($e^{C_2}$ class), and $\Delta M(s)\,\omega = \Mdot s\,\omega$
carries $s/\tau_{\rm end}\le1$ (also nondecreasing) times one, so it
too is $e^{C_2}$ class. For the \emph{nominal} trajectory the lemma
is therefore exact, with no safety factor.

\emph{Nominal to true.} The true trajectory multiplies each factor by
$1 + O(e/\omega_{\rm nom})$. Measured on the exact nonlinear tip-over
across the seven rates, the worst excess of a true ratio over its
nominal bound is $2.6\%$, at the slowest ramp near the window end;
the $1.05$ carried on $\dot\rho_2$ in (16) is that transfer
allowance, held with double margin. The safety factor thus covers only
the nominal-to-true step --- the shape itself is proved.

\emph{Step 6 (optional tightening, factor $8$) --- use the decay of
$\dot\rho$.} The forcing is not constant: it peaks at the window end
and decays backwards as marked in (16) and proved in Step 5$'$.
So solve (14) once for each shape at unit peak --- the same two-line
comparison applies, and the solutions are still elementary. In the
scaled variable $u = C_2(\tau - \tau_{\rm end}) \in [-\xe, 0]$ ---
a \emph{translation}, not a reflection: $du/d\tau = +C_2 > 0$, so
$u$ runs forward with $\tau$ and only the origin has moved to the
window end (a reflection would be $C_2(\tau_{\rm end}-\tau)$; the
``backward'' of Step 5$'$ is how the envelope is \emph{described},
not the orientation of the coordinate) --- for the forcing
$e^{2C_2(\tau-\tau_{\rm end})} = e^{2u}$:
\begin{equation}
  w_2(\tau) \;=\; \frac{\dot\rho_2}{J_PC_2^2}\cdot\frac13
  \left[\frac{\sinh(u+\xe)}{\sinh \xe}
  + e^{-2\xe}\,\frac{\sinh(-u)}{\sinh \xe} - e^{2u}\right],
  \qquad
  M_2(\xe) \;\triangleq\; \max_{u\in[-\xe,0]}\frac13\big[\,\cdots\big] ,
  \tag{17a}
\end{equation}
and for the forcing $e^{C_2(\tau-\tau_{\rm end})} = e^{u}$, which is
\emph{resonant} --- it decays at the operator's own rate, so the
particular solution carries a factor of $u$:
\begin{equation}
  w_1(\tau) \;=\; \frac{\dot\rho_1}{J_PC_2^2}
  \left[\frac{\xe}{2}\,\frac{\sinh(u+\xe)}{\sinh \xe}
  - \frac{(u+\xe)\,e^{u}}{2}\right],
  \qquad
  M_1(\xe) \;\triangleq\; \max_{u\in[-\xe,0]}\big[\,\cdots\big] .
  \tag{17b}
\end{equation}
No time reversal hides in (17a): substituting $u = C_2\tau - \xe$
back gives, in original time,
\begin{equation*}
  w_2(\tau) = \frac{\dot\rho_2}{J_PC_2^2}\cdot\frac13
  \left[\frac{\sinh(C_2\tau)}{\sinh\xe}
  + e^{-2\xe}\,\frac{\sinh(C_2(\tau_{\rm end}-\tau))}{\sinh\xe}
  - e^{2C_2(\tau-\tau_{\rm end})}\right],
\end{equation*}
which vanishes at $\tau = 0$ ($0 + e^{-2\xe} - e^{-2\xe}$) and at
$\tau = \tau_{\rm end}$ ($1 + 0 - 1$), with the forcing term growing
forward in $\tau$ to its peak at the end --- exactly the orientation
of the physical window. (Backward time $v = \tau_{\rm end}-\tau$
would give the mirror parametrisation $w(\tau_{\rm end}-v)$ with
forcing $e^{-2C_2v}$ --- the same function, described from the other
end.)

\emph{Where the two forms come from --- construction, not guess.} In
the scaled variable the comparison problem reads $w'' - w =
-\kappa\,g(u)$, $w(-\xe) = w(0) = 0$, with $\kappa =
\dot\rho_i/(J_PC_2^2)$, one shape $g$ at a time. (One label trap,
worth naming: these are the \emph{same} two conditions as
$w(\tau{=}0) = w(\tau_{\rm end}) = 0$, relabelled --- since the
origin of $u$ sits at the window end, the token ``$0$'' names the
\emph{onset} in $\tau$-labels but the \emph{window end} in
$u$-labels: $u = -\xe$ is $\tau = 0$, and $u = 0$ is
$\tau = \tau_{\rm end}$. Two physical pins, onset and end, in either
language.) Three moves each.
\emph{Particular solution:} for $g = e^{2u}$ try $Ae^{2u}$:
$(2^2 - 1)A = -\kappa$, so $A = -\kappa/3$ --- the $1/3$ is the
distance to resonance. For $g = e^{u}$ the same try fails,
$(1^2 - 1)A = 0$: the forcing decays at the operator's own rate, so
carry a factor $u$, $w_p = -(\kappa/2)\,u e^{u}$, since
$(ue^u)'' - ue^u = 2e^u$ --- this extra $u$ is why $M_1 > M_2$.
\emph{Homogeneous corrections in the endpoint basis:} $\sinh(u+\xe)$
vanishes at $u = -\xe$ and $\sinh(-u)$ vanishes at $u = 0$, so each
boundary condition sets one coefficient without disturbing the other:
$u = 0$ gives $a\sinh \xe  = \kappa/3$, $u = -\xe$ gives $b\sinh \xe  =
(\kappa/3)e^{-2\xe}$ --- that is (17a); for $e^{u}$, collecting the two
$u$-terms into $-(u+\xe)e^{u}/2$ makes the zero at $u = -\xe$ visible ---
that is (17b). \emph{Verification by substitution:} the $\sinh$'s are
annihilated by the operator, the exponentials return the forcing, and
both brackets vanish at both ends --- the two boundary zeros of (14).
Their maxima barely depend on $\xe$ and tend to simple constants,
\begin{equation}
  M_2(\xe)\to\tfrac1{12},
  \qquad
  M_1(\xe)\to\tfrac1{2e},
  \qquad\text{so}\qquad
  |\de| \;\le\; \frac{M_2\dot\rho_2 + M_1\dot\rho_1}{Wz_{CoM}} .
  \tag{17}
\end{equation}
The limits are one-line maximisations: as $\xe  \to \infty$,
$\sinh(u+\xe)/\sinh \xe  \to e^{u}$ and the $e^{-2\xe}$ term dies, so the
bracket of (17a) tends to $(e^{u} - e^{2u})/3 = (v - v^2)/3$ with
$v = e^{u} \in (0,1]$, maximised at $v = \tfrac12$ to $1/12$
($\ln 2$ e-foldings before the window end); the bracket of (17b)
tends to $-(u/2)e^{u}$, maximised at $u = -1$ to $1/(2e)$ (one
e-folding before the end). (Over the campaign range
$\xe  = 1.9$--$3.7$: $M_2 = 0.074$--$0.083$, $M_1 = 0.141$--$0.181$;
the script evaluates (17a)--(17b) exactly.)
The interpretation is the same as the manuscript's reduction factors
$1/7$ and $1/5$: the forcing is largest exactly where the pinned
response is smallest --- at the window end the boundary zero sits
right under the peak of the load.

\emph{A one-line version, in the spirit of (85).} The limits are
also \emph{ceilings}: $M_2(\xe)$ and $M_1(\xe)$ approach $1/12$ and
$1/(2e)$ from below (verified numerically over $\xe  \in [0.05, 12]$ to
grid precision), so (17) may be quoted $\xe$-free,
\begin{equation*}
  |\de| \;\le\;
  \frac{\dot\rho_2/12 \;+\; \dot\rho_1/(2e)}{Wz_{CoM}} ,
\end{equation*}
with no $M$ curves, no window length, and no $C_2$ beyond what sits
inside $\dot\rho_{1,2}$ --- exactly as (85) trades the exact
remainder for $\tfrac12 G''(0)\varphi^2$. Rerun on the $140$ runs,
the constant version costs at most $15\%$ on the worst run ($0.2$--$7\%$
in the per-rate mean, largest at the fastest ramps where $\xe$ is
smallest), keeps $140/140$ coverage, and the worst-run used fraction
rises only to $0.86$. Note where the simplification is \emph{not}
available: the channel amplitudes $\dot\rho_{1,2}$ are already the
$\varphi^\ast = 0$ worst-case Taylor coefficients of (85) --- nothing
further to drop there --- and the time \emph{shape} behind $M_2, M_1$
(the forcing concentrated at the window end as $e^{2C_2(s-\tau_{\rm
end})}$) cannot be dropped at all: against a constant-in-time forcing
the comparison factor is $1 - \mathrm{sech}(\xe/2) = 0.4$--$0.9$, a
factor $5$--$10$ above $1/12$, which is the loose bound this document
exists to escape. The Taylor step simplifies the \emph{amplitude};
the tightness lives in the \emph{shape}.

\section{The pre-onset segment}

\emph{Where the segment comes from.} By (9), $\dtc < 0$: the witness
branch starts $|\dtc|$ \emph{before} the true onset. On the segment
$\tau\in[\dtc,\,0]$ --- length $|\dtc|$ --- the record is still at its
baseline, but the branch has already been running for
$u = \tau - \dtc \in [0, |\dtc|]$ since its own onset, so the mismatch
there is the branch itself:
\begin{equation}
  \text{mismatch}(u) \;=\; C_1\big(\cosh(C_2u)-1\big),
  \qquad u \in [0,\,|\dtc|] .
  \tag{18a}
\end{equation}
The residual sum runs over this segment too, so the witness's cost in
(10) gains its square. Normalising by the main window length
$\tau_{\rm end}$ (conservative: the extended window is longer, so
dividing by the shorter $\tau_{\rm end}$ can only enlarge the term),
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{\rm pre}^2 \;=\; \frac{1}{\tau_{\rm end}}
  \int_0^{|\dtc|}\big[C_1\big(\cosh(C_2u)-1\big)\big]^2\,du .
  \tag{18b}
\end{equation}
The segment's \emph{noise} adds no separate term: $\hat n$ is an RMS
density, unchanged by a slightly longer window.

\emph{The integral is elementary.} Expand the square with
$\cosh^2 a = \tfrac12(1+\cosh 2a)$:
\begin{equation}
  \big(\cosh a - 1\big)^2
  = \cosh^2 a - 2\cosh a + 1
  = \tfrac32 + \tfrac12\cosh(2a) - 2\cosh a ,
  \tag{18c}
\end{equation}
and integrate term by term with $a = C_2u$
($\tfrac32 \to \tfrac{3u}2$;\ \
$\tfrac12\cosh(2C_2u) \to \sinh(2C_2u)/(4C_2)$;\ \
$-2\cosh(C_2u) \to -2\sinh(C_2u)/C_2$):
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{\rm pre}^2 \;=\; \frac{C_1^2}{\tau_{\rm end}}
  \left[\frac{3u}{2} + \frac{\sinh(2C_2u)}{4C_2}
  - \frac{2\sinh(C_2u)}{C_2}\right]_{0}^{|\dtc|} .
  \tag{18}
\end{equation}

\emph{Check, and why the term is small.} Expanding the bracket in
powers of $u$, the $u$ and $u^3$ orders cancel identically and the
first surviving order is
\begin{equation*}
  \int_0^{|\dtc|}\big(\cosh(C_2u)-1\big)^2 du
  \;\approx\; \frac{C_2^4\,|\dtc|^5}{20} ,
\end{equation*}
fifth order in the shift, because the branch leaves the baseline only
quadratically ($\cosh - 1 \approx C_2^2u^2/2$, squared $\to u^4$).
Bounding instead by (segment sup)$\times$(length) would give
$|\dtc|^5/4$ --- the exact integral is a factor $\sqrt5\approx2.2$
tighter. (The branch amplitude is $R \le C_1$; using $C_1$ in (18)
errs on the high side.) Evaluated with the a priori $|\dtc|$ from (9)
and $e_\omega(\tau_{\rm end}) \le E(\tau_{\rm end})$:
$\Delta_{\rm pre} = 0.036^\circ$/s at $\Mdot = 0.10$, $0.011$ at
$0.20$, and below $0.006$ beyond.

\emph{The baseline constant $R-C_1$ --- both bookkeepings.} One
witness must serve every sample the minimiser's cost touches, and its
baseline slot holds a single number, so the constant $R-C_1$ of (7)
must land somewhere. There are two consistent choices. \emph{(a)}
Take (3) literally, $C = b$: the flat pre-onset samples then cost
nothing beyond noise, (18a) holds as written (amplitude $R \le C_1$),
and the post-onset residual gains the constant,
$r = \de + n + (C_1 - R)$. \emph{(b)} Take $C = b + (R - C_1)$: the
post-onset identity is exactly (5), $r = \de + n$, and the constant
moves to the flat stretch, where the length fraction of that stretch
in the window dilutes it further. Either way the price is the same
number,
\begin{equation*}
  C_1 - R \;=\; C_1\Big(1 - \sqrt{1 - (\beta/C_1)^2}\Big)
  \;\le\; \tfrac12\,C_1\,(\beta/C_1)^2 ,
\end{equation*}
second order in $\beta/C_1$. Realised ($\beta/C_1 \le 0.023$) it is
below $0.004^\circ$/s on every run --- two orders under $\hat n$ and
below even $\Delta_{\rm pre}$ --- which is why (10) and (20) carry it
silently; the pre-onset median cannot even distinguish $b$ from
$b + (R-C_1)$ at that size. At the a priori envelope ceiling it is
not invisible: $u = 0.59$ at the slowest ramp gives
$C_1(1-\sqrt{1-u^2}) \approx 0.2^\circ$/s, about a fifth of the model
term there. Adding it to the cap only loosens the bound, so the
$140/140$ check is unaffected either way, and the measured
$\beta/C_1$, thirty times below the envelope, confirms the silent
treatment a posteriori.

\section{The noise term}

Above $5$ Hz the model can produce nothing, so $n_{\rm hi}$ is
measured from the record; the full band is estimated through the
quiet-window shape ratio,
\begin{equation}
  \hat n \;=\; \rms(n_{\rm hi})\sqrt{1 + (s_{\rm med}\,\kappa_q)^2},
  \tag{19}
\end{equation}
with $s_{\rm med} = 1.60$ the campaign-median in-window enhancement
and $\kappa_q$ the run's own quiet ratio (campaign median $0.37$ where
no quiet stretch exists).

\emph{The cutoff is not delicate, and $k\sigma$ is what (19) already
is.} Structurally (19) reads $k\cdot\sigma$ with
$\sigma = \rms(n_{\rm hi})$ --- measured \emph{in the window}, in a
band the model cannot contaminate --- and
$k = \sqrt{1+(s_{\rm med}\kappa_q)^2} \approx 1.6$--$1.9$. The split
frequency only defines that band, and any choice works that sits well
above the model's spectral content $C_2/2\pi = 0.6$--$0.9$ Hz and
leaves spectral bins on both sides ($f_c \gtrsim 2/(NT_s) \approx 3$
Hz for the shortest windows): recomputing the entire check at
$f_c = 2$, $3$ and $5$ Hz gives identical results ($140/140$ inside,
mean used $0.57$--$0.58$), and only past $8$ Hz does the shrinking
high band make the estimate noisy ($133/140$ at $8$, $129/140$ at
$10$). The invariance is expected: $\hat n$ estimates the total
$\rms(n)$, which does not depend on where the record is split. What
does \emph{not} work is the simpler $k\sigma$ with $\sigma$ from the
pre-onset segment: the in-window disturbance is contact-borne ---
pivoting on one gear edge, not gyro noise --- and runs $2$ to $50$
times the pre-onset floor with no usable floor at all on $25$ of the
$140$ runs, so the one campaign constant that covers the spread,
$k = 49$, collapses the used fraction from $0.58$ to $0.16$.

\section{The two answers this gives}

\emph{Why the residual is flat in $\Mdot$.} Nothing in (17) grows with
the window --- $M_2$, $M_1$ are constants and the channel sups barely
move --- so the theory \emph{predicts} a rate-flat residual, and the
data agrees: the measured $\rms(r)$ stays at $0.95$--$1.08^\circ$/s
across a twelvefold change in $\Mdot$ (correlation with $\Mdot$:
$r = +0.09$, $p = 0.31$). The envelope (90), which grows like
$\sinh \xe$, bounds $e_\omega$ --- not the residual --- and comparing
the residual against it is what made the old cap look loose.

\emph{How far the shift moves the critical moment.} The absorbed shift
is (9), and the moment error it causes cancels $\Mdot$ exactly:
\begin{equation}
  \big|\Delta M_{\rm crit}\big| \;=\; \Mdot\,|\dtc|
  \;\approx\; \Mdot\cdot
  \frac{e_\omega(\tau_{\rm end})}{C_1C_2\sinh \xe}
  \;=\; \frac{Wz_{CoM}\,e_\omega(\tau_{\rm end})}{C_2\sinh \xe}
  \;\le\; \frac{Wz_{CoM}\,E(\tau_{\rm end})}{C_2\sinh \xe}
  \;=\; \rbar ,
  \tag{21}
\end{equation}
using $\Mdot/C_1 = Wz_{CoM}$ and $E(\tau_{\rm end}) =
\rbar\sinh \xe/(J_PC_2)$. So the shift can displace the identified
threshold by at most the window-averaged remainder --- the same
$\rbar$ the manuscript already budgets --- and measured it uses
$0.36$--$1.85$ mN\,m of the $\approx12$ allowed, the sign lowering
$M_{\rm crit}$ (earlier onset). (The chain's middle step is the
first-order $|\dtc|$; exactly,
$|\dtc| = \operatorname{artanh}(\beta/C_1)/C_2$, so the ceiling
acquires a factor $\operatorname{artanh}(u)/u$ with $u = \beta/C_1$:
$1.0002$ on the campaign (realised $u \le 0.023$), up to $1.13$ at
the design-box corner of Sec.~9 where the a priori $u$ of (9a)
reaches $0.66$.)

\section*{Remarks}

\emph{(a) Without the differential equation.} The same $\de$ can be
written directly from the manuscript's Duhamel form (89), giving
$\de = \tfrac1{J_P}\int_0^{\tau_{\rm end}}K(\tau,s)\rho(s)\,ds$ with a
kernel that the addition identities collapse to
$K = \sinh(C_2(\tau_{\rm end}-\tau))\cosh(C_2 s)/\sinh(C_2\tau_{\rm
end})$ for $s\le\tau$ (minus its mirror for $s>\tau$), and
$\max|K| = 1$ exactly --- the exponential cancels inside the kernel.
It is an instructive identity, but as a bound it is weaker: $K$
changes sign at $s=\tau$, so only $\int|K|$ can be used, and that
costs a factor $\approx2.4$ over (17). The derivative bound in (16)
is what forbids $\rho$ from flipping sign with $K$, and that
smoothness is worth exactly that factor.

\emph{(b) What is a priori and what is not.} The model term of (20)
uses only pre-experiment constants (list under (16)). The noise term
(19) is measured: $n_{\rm hi}$ from the run itself, $\kappa_q$ from
quiet stretches, $s_{\rm med}$ one campaign median. A fully
pre-experiment cap replaces $\hat n$ with a bench noise figure.

\emph{(c) Exponent mismatch.} If the true exponent differs from the
pinned $C_2$ by a relative $\varepsilon_2$, the mismatch joins
$e_\omega$ by definition and its unabsorbed part costs
$0.018$--$0.022^\circ$/s per $1\%$ of $\varepsilon_2$ --- $5$--$8\%$
of the model term, and the calibration residuals limit
$\varepsilon_2$ to a few percent.

\section*{Appendix: the linear-algebra picture}

Collect the $N$ samples of a signal into a vector in $\mathbb R^N$.
The model family (2) is then a subset of that space,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal M \;=\;
  \Big\{\, a\big(\cosh(c(\tau_i - d)) - 1\big) + b
  \;:\; a, c > 0,\ d, b \in \mathbb R \,\Big\}
  \;\subset\; \mathbb R^N ,
  \tag{A.1}
\end{equation}
a four-parameter curved manifold. Its tangent space at the nominal is
spanned by the four derivative directions
\begin{equation}
  T \;=\; \operatorname{span}\big\{\,
  \underbrace{\cosh(C_2\tau)-1}_{\partial/\partial C_1},\;
  \underbrace{\sinh(C_2\tau)}_{\partial/\partial\dtc},\;
  \underbrace{\tau\sinh(C_2\tau)}_{\partial/\partial C_2},\;
  \underbrace{\mathbf 1}_{\partial/\partial C}
  \,\big\},
  \tag{A.2}
\end{equation}
so the family can move in at most four of the $N$ dimensions around
any of its points.

\emph{Why exact representation fails.} ``The true curve is a shifted
nominal'' asserts membership: $y_{\rm struct}\in\mathcal M$. Two
obstructions, matching the two in the Sec.~4 remark. Dimension: a
four-dimensional manifold is a measure-zero sliver of $\mathbb R^N$;
a vector off it stays off it. Dynamics, sharper: every element of
$\mathcal M$ samples a homogeneous solution of
$\ddot v = c^2 v$, while $y_{\rm struct}$ samples the forced equation
(12); by the argument under (13), membership would force
$\dot\rho\equiv0$. So $y_{\rm struct}$ has a component
\emph{transverse} to $\mathcal M$ that no parameter choice reaches.

\emph{What introducing $\de$ does.} It changes the assertion from
membership to a coordinate statement:
\begin{equation}
  y_{\rm struct} \;=\; m \;+\; \de,
  \qquad m \in \mathcal M ,
  \tag{A.3}
\end{equation}
exact for \emph{every} choice of $m$ --- the freedom is in where to
put the remainder, not whether there is one. Two canonical choices
divide the labour:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
choice of $m$ & property of $\de$ & what it buys\\
\midrule
orthogonal projection (LS) & $\de \perp T$, minimal norm &
  the smallest remainder, no pointwise structure\\
end-matched witness, (9) & $\de = 0$ at both window ends &
  the Dirichlet problem (13)--(14), hence (17)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The witness pays a factor $1.8$--$2.0$ in norm over the orthogonal
choice and buys, in exchange, the two boundary zeros that make the
maximum principle --- and with it the entire a priori bound ---
available. Provability is purchased with norm.

\emph{The projector sub-case, and the manuscript's Appendix C.} With
$C_2$ pinned and the onset held, the family linearises to the subspace
$\mathcal V = \operatorname{span}\{u, \mathbf 1\}$,
$u_i = \cosh(C_2\tau_i)-1$, and the orthogonal choice becomes an
explicit matrix: with $A = [\,u\ \ \mathbf 1\,]$,
\begin{equation}
  P = A(A^{\!\top}\!A)^{-1}A^{\!\top},
  \qquad
  P_{ij} = \frac1N + \frac{\tilde u_i\tilde u_j}{\sum_k \tilde u_k^2},
  \qquad
  r_\perp = (I-P)(e_\omega + n),
  \tag{A.4}
\end{equation}
($\tilde u = u - \bar u$) --- the manuscript's Lemma~2, eq.~(C3). The
$\de$ of this document is the same object one level up: the remainder
transverse to the full nonlinear family rather than to its pinned
linearisation.

\emph{How much of $e_\omega$ lies along the family.} Measured on the
exact tip-over, the fraction of $e_\omega$ (in RMS) absorbed by
successively larger reachable sets: $61$--$67\%$ by
$\operatorname{span}\{u,\mathbf 1\}$, $70$--$71\%$ adding the onset
direction $\sinh$, $92.5\%$ by the full four-parameter family --- the
last flat across all rates. The transverse component --- the $7.5\%$
no parameters reach, $0.012$--$0.018^\circ$/s --- is the irreducible
core of $\de$, and (17) is its a priori ceiling.
