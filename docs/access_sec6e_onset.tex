Because the measured angular rate $y(t)$ can be regarded as the nominal
response perturbed by the unmodelled forcing $\rho$, the minimiser
selects $\hat\tc$ by minimising the two-segment cost over the record.
Both segments enter, and both share the pre-onset level $C$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  J(\tc) = &\int_{t_0}^{\tc}\bigl(y(t) - C\bigr)^2dt \\
    &+ \int_{\tc}^{\tend}
       \Bigl(y(t) - \bigl(\hat\omega(t;\tc) + C\bigr)\Bigr)^2dt .
\end{aligned}
  \tag{98}
\end{equation}

Write the recorded rate as the true pre-onset level, the nominal
response from the true onset, and a deviation:
\begin{equation}
  y(t) = b + \hat\omega(t;\tcs) + e_\omega(t),
  \qquad
  \hat\tc = \tcs + \delta \tc .
  \tag{99}
\end{equation}
Here $b$ is the rate the vehicle actually holds before the excitation,
zero under the onset conditions. The rate is taken from the navigation
filter, whose delta-angle bias states remove the gyro bias, so $b$
represents residual pre-excitation motion rather than sensor bias; $C$
is its estimate, formed over the pre-onset segment. Note also that
$e_\omega$ is anchored at the \emph{true} onset $\tcs$: once a run is
recorded it is a fixed function of $t$, and it carries no dependence on
the optimisation variable.

A first-order expansion of the estimated rate gives
\begin{equation}
  \hat\omega\bigl(t;\hat\tc\bigr)
  \;\simeq\; \hat\omega\bigl(t;\tcs\bigr) + \delta\tc\,\chi(t)
  \tag{100}
\end{equation}
with the sensitivity
\begin{equation}
  \chi(t) \triangleq
  \frac{\partial\hat\omega(t;\tc)}{\partial \tc}\bigg|_{\tc = \tcs}
  = -\,C_1C_2\sinh C_2\bigl(t - \tcs\bigr).
  \tag{101}
\end{equation}

Two features of (98) make the differentiation clean, and both are worth
stating because each looks like an omission otherwise.

First, $\tc$ is the upper limit of one integral and the lower limit of
the other, so the Leibniz rule produces two boundary terms. They cancel
identically. The rise contributes $C_1(\cosh 0 - 1) = 0$ at $t = \tc$,
so the two segments agree there and both terms equal
$\bigl(y(\tc) - C\bigr)^2$ with opposite signs. Nothing is discarded,
and the cancellation is exact rather than asymptotic.

Second, substituting (99) and (100), the post-onset residual is
\begin{equation}
  y(t) - \hat\omega(t;\hat\tc) - C
  = e_\omega(t) - \delta\tc\,\chi(t) - (C - b),
  \tag{102}
\end{equation}
so $C$ enters only through the baseline \emph{error} $C - b$, not
through its own size. A large but correctly estimated pre-onset level
is harmless.

Differentiating (98) with respect to $\delta\tc$ --- the same operator
as $\partial/\partial\tc$ by (99), but the variable in which the
linearised cost is an exact quadratic --- and setting the result to zero
gives, when the pre-onset fit is unbiased,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial J}{\partial\,\delta\tc}
  = -2\int_{\tc}^{\tend}\bigl(e_\omega(t) - \delta\tc\,\chi(t)\bigr)
    \chi(t)\,dt = 0 ,
  \tag{103}
\end{equation}
which implies
\begin{equation}
  \delta\tc = \frac{\langle e_\omega,\chi\rangle}{\|\chi\|^{2}} .
  \tag{104}
\end{equation}
Here $\langle a,b\rangle$ denotes the $L^2$ inner product over the
fitting window $[\tcs,\tend]$, i.e.\ $\langle a,b\rangle \triangleq
\int_{\tcs}^{\tend}a(t)b(t)\,dt$, and $\|b\|^2 \triangleq \langle
b,b\rangle$. The shift $\delta\tc$ is well defined because the
maximum-angle event occurs after the onset, $\tau_{\rm end} > 0$, so
$\sinh C_2\tau$ does not vanish identically and $\|\chi\|^2 > 0$.

\paragraph{The baseline error, retained.}
Keeping the last term of (102) rather than assuming it away replaces
(104) by
\begin{equation}
  \delta\tc = \frac{\langle e_\omega,\chi\rangle}{\|\chi\|^{2}}
   \;-\; (C - b)\,\frac{\langle 1,\chi\rangle}{\|\chi\|^{2}} ,
  \tag{105}
\end{equation}
and the second term does not vanish: $\chi$ is of one sign, so
$\langle1,\chi\rangle = -C_1(\cosh x - 1)$. Evaluating it with
$\|\chi\|^2 = C_1^2C_2\bigl[\tfrac14\sinh 2x - \tfrac12 x\bigr]$ and
carrying it through $\Delta M_{\rm crit} = \dot M\,\delta\tc$, the
amplitude $C_1$ cancels as it does elsewhere and
\begin{equation}
  \Delta M_{{\rm crit},C}
  = \Wz\,(C - b)\,
    \frac{\cosh x - 1}
         {C_2\bigl[\tfrac14\sinh 2x - \tfrac12 x\bigr]} .
  \tag{106}
\end{equation}
Over the operating box of Sec.~VI-D the gain of (106) runs from
$0.042$~N\,m per rad/s at the slowest ramp to $0.351$ at the fastest,
the fast end being worse because the window is shorter. What the fitted
baselines actually are, and how far the identified offset moves when
$C$ is pinned to zero instead of fitted, are measurements and are
reported with the realised budget in Sec.~VIII-E.

\paragraph{From the onset shift to the threshold.}
Substituting the Duhamel integral (88) for $e_\omega$ in (104) and
exchanging the order of integration --- Fubini, both integrands being
continuous on a bounded domain, the inner limits changing from
$s\in[0,\tau]$ to $\tau\in[s,\tau_{\rm end}]$ --- turns the correlation
into an average of $\rho$ against a fixed weight:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta M_{{\rm crit},e} = -\!\int_0^{\tau_{\rm end}}\!\rho(s)\,w(s)\,ds,
  \quad
  w(s) = \frac{\Wz\!\displaystyle\int_s^{\tau_{\rm end}}\!\!
                \sinh(C_2\tau)\cosh\bigl(C_2(\tau-s)\bigr)d\tau}
              {J_PC_2\,\bigl\|\sinh(C_2\tau)\bigr\|^{2}} .
  \tag{107}
\end{equation}

Three properties of $w$ close the argument. It is non-negative, being an
integral of hyperbolic functions of non-negative arguments. It
integrates to one \emph{exactly}: the same exchange over the triangle
$0\le s\le\tau\le\tau_{\rm end}$ reproduces in the numerator the very
norm that sits in the denominator, leaving $\Wz/(J_PC_2^{2}) = 1$ by
(20). And it is non-increasing in $s$, since both the integration range
and the $\cosh$ factor shrink as $s$ grows.

The first two properties alone give
$|\Delta M_{{\rm crit},e}| \le \langle|\rho|\rangle_w$, the
$w$-weighted average of $|\rho|$, since $w\ge0$ and $\int w = 1$. The
third admits Chebyshev's inequality --- a forcing that rises against a
weight that falls, the same pairing as before --- and replaces that
average by the one already bounded at (95).

It must be applied to the two channels \emph{separately}, and the reason
is not cosmetic: $\rho$ itself does not rise. Near the onset
$\varphi\sim\tau^3$ makes $\rho_\varphi\propto\varphi^2$ of order
$\tau^6$ while $\rho_{GE}\propto\dot M\tau\varphi$ is of order $\tau^4$,
so the ground-effect term dominates there; being restoring, it holds
$\rho$ \emph{negative} over the first $13$ to $24\%$ of the window
(integrating the exact nonlinear tip-over, Sec.~VIII). Taken on their
own the two channels are monotone throughout: $\rho_\varphi\ge0$ rises
because $\varphi$ does, and $|\rho_{GE}| = |\beta_M|\dot M\tau\varphi$
rises for the same reason. Applying the inequality to each and adding,
\begin{equation}
  \bigl|\Delta M_{{\rm crit},e}\bigr|
  \;\le\; \langle\rho_\varphi\rangle_w + \langle|\rho_{GE}|\rangle_w
  \;\le\; \bar\rho
  \;\le\; \tfrac17\,\rho_{\varphi,\max} + \tfrac15\,\rho_{GE,\max} .
  \tag{108}
\end{equation}
The two reduction factors are therefore produced by the step that uses
them, rather than imported past a hypothesis the sum does not satisfy.
Note that $\bar\rho$ is the average defined at (95), and the right-hand
side bounds it rather than defining it. The constants are the reduction
factors themselves, not the factors multiplied by a kernel gain, because
the onset is located by a \emph{normalised} correlation: $\int w = 1$
leaves nothing over.

That each channel rises across the window is what makes the
Chebyshev step available, and it should be read as a hypothesis that
happens to hold rather than as a conservatism. The weight is
\emph{largest at the onset}: $w(0) = C_2\sinh^2\!x\,/\,2\bigl[\tfrac14
\sinh 2x - \tfrac12 x\bigr]$, which is $5.16$~s$^{-1}$ at
$\dot M = 1.20$~N\,m/s against a mean of $1.63$, and it falls
monotonically from there. Forcing that acts early is therefore weighted
\emph{most}, not least, and it is only because
$\rho_\varphi\propto\varphi^{2}$ and $\rho_{GE}\propto\tau\varphi$ both
vanish at the onset and rise afterwards that the pairing runs the
favourable way. Reversing the arrangement reverses the conclusion:
holding the window mean fixed at $\bar\rho$ and letting $\rho$
\emph{fall} across the window instead, the identified threshold moves
by $1.4$ to $1.6\,\bar\rho$, outside (108). The monotonicity is doing
real work and is not a technicality.

It is tempting to argue instead that an early perturbation cannot
displace the onset at all, on the grounds that expanding the kernel of
(88) as $\cosh C_2(\tau-s) = \cosh C_2\tau\cosh C_2 s - \sinh C_2\tau
\sinh C_2 s$ leaves the $\sinh$ half weighted by $\sinh C_2s$, which
vanishes at $s = 0$. That reasoning assigns the $\cosh$ half to the
amplitude, and the amplitude is pinned, so it is not available to take
it. What the $\cosh$ half does instead is project onto the onset
direction, and it projects strongly: over this window $\cosh C_2\tau$
and $\sinh C_2\tau$ are separated by only $3.4^\circ$ once the constant
is removed, so $99.8\%$ of an amplitude perturbation lies along the
directions the estimator can move in. A pure $2\%$ amplitude error,
with no onset error whatever, displaces the identified threshold by
$4.3$~mN\,m --- a third of the budget of (108) --- while changing the
fit residual by $0.024^\circ$/s. Equation (107) already accounts for
this, being derived from the projection rather than from matching
coefficients, which is why $w(0)$ comes out as the maximum.

The constants differ between the two halves of (97) because the two
questions do. The first asks how far the fitted rate curve is
displaced, so the kernel integral $\Psi$ multiplies $\bar\rho$ and the
products climb to $\tfrac12$ and $1$; the second asks how far the
\emph{minimiser} moves, and by (107) that is a normalised correlation
with no kernel gain left over, so $\bar\rho$ enters bare and the
constants are the reduction factors themselves. The threshold bound is
the stronger of the two by about a factor of four, which is why it,
and not the relative rate bound, is what the offset budget inherits. A
reviewer can evaluate either from numbers already in the paper.

\paragraph{What (108) covers.}
Only the displacement driven by the forcing the estimator cannot absorb.
Two further contributions sit outside it and are measured rather than
bounded a priori. The baseline error enters through (106), so that
$|\Delta M_{\rm crit}| \le \bar\rho + |\Delta M_{{\rm crit},C}|$. And
the applied moment departs from the linear ramp the model assumes:
because $\dot M$ is the least-squares slope of the \emph{measured}
moment over the window, the constant and linear parts of any tracking
error are removed by the normal equations, but the non-affine remainder
survives and pairs with the non-affine part of $w$. Both are reported
with the realised budget in Sec.~VIII-E, alongside the ground-contact
geometry that dominates all three.
