\subsection{What is to be proved, and what is not true}

Write the recorded rate over the post-onset window as the nominal
response, the deviation the model does not carry, and sensor noise,
\begin{equation}
  y(\tau) = \omega_{\rm nom}(\tau) + e_\omega(\tau) + n(\tau),
  \tag{P.1}
\end{equation}
and let $\hat\omega$ be the fitted curve, chosen to minimise
$\|y-g\|^2$ over the model family. The natural statement to want about
the residual $r = y - \hat\omega$ is
\begin{equation}
  |r(\tau)| \;\overset{?}{\le}\; |e_\omega(\tau)| + k\sigma_n .
  \tag{P.2}
\end{equation}
It is false, and the counterexample is at the onset. The envelope of
(93) vanishes there, $E(\tau)\propto\sinh C_2\tau\to0$, and so does
$e_\omega$; the residual does not. Minimisation is over the whole
window, so a deviation living at large $\tau$ moves the fitted curve at
$\tau = 0$ as well, and (P.2) would require that movement to vanish
where $e_\omega$ does. No multiplicative form $|r|\le\Gamma|e_\omega| +
k\sigma_n$ can hold either, for the same reason: measured on the
campaign the ratio of the true bound to $E$ is $2.1$ at the end of the
window and $27$ to $95$ at the first sample.

What is missing is the displacement of the fit itself. It costs one
term, and --- this is the point of the section --- that term is
expressible in $e_\omega$ and $n$ alone, so the statement stays in the
form (P.2) asks for.

\subsection{The proof}

Three steps. The first two are where the work is and neither needs more
than (VIII.3) already assumes.

\paragraph{Step 1: optimality bounds the displacement, in norm.} The
nominal is itself a member of the family --- it is a hyperbolic cosine
with the true amplitude and exponent --- so the minimiser cannot be
farther from the data than it is. With the triangle inequality,
\begin{equation}
  \bigl\|\hat\omega - \omega_{\rm nom}\bigr\|
  \;\le\; \|\hat\omega - y\| + \|y - \omega_{\rm nom}\|
  \;\le\; 2\,\|e_\omega + n\| ,
  \tag{P.3}
\end{equation}
the second step using $\|\hat\omega - y\| = \min_g\|g-y\| \le
\|\omega_{\rm nom} - y\|$. Nothing here is linear: (P.3) holds for any
family containing $\omega_{\rm nom}$ and any global minimiser over it.

When the family \emph{is} linear the factor drops to one. The estimator
holds $C_2$ at the value fixed by the rig and fits the amplitude and the
pre-onset level, so on the sample grid $\tau_1,\dots,\tau_N$ the
admissible curves form a two-dimensional subspace
\begin{equation}
  V = \operatorname{span}\{u,\mathbf 1\} = \operatorname{range}A,
  \quad
  A = \bigl[\,u\;\;\mathbf 1\,\bigr]\in\reals^{N\times2},
  \quad u_i = \cosh C_2\tau_i - 1 ,
  \tag{P.4}
\end{equation}
the minimiser is $\hat\omega = Py$ with $P = A(A^{\!\top}\!A)^{-1}
A^{\!\top}$, and since $\omega_{\rm nom}\in V$ is fixed by $P$,
\begin{equation}
  \hat\omega - \omega_{\rm nom} = P\bigl(e_\omega + n\bigr),
  \qquad
  \bigl\|\hat\omega - \omega_{\rm nom}\bigr\| \le \|e_\omega + n\| .
  \tag{P.5}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Step 2: in a finite-dimensional space, a norm bounds a
value.} The displacement lies in $V$, and for $v\in V$ we have $v = Pv$,
so $v_i = \langle P_{i\cdot},v\rangle$ and, since
$\|P_{i\cdot}\|^2 = (P^2)_{ii} = P_{ii}$,
\begin{equation}
  |v_i| \;\le\; \sqrt{P_{ii}}\;\|v\| .
  \tag{P.6}
\end{equation}
This is exact, not an estimate, and $\sqrt{P_{ii}}$ is computable
before a run is flown. It is the classical leverage of sample $i$; over
the campaign $\max_i\sqrt{P_{ii}} = 0.35$ to $0.40$.

\paragraph{Step 3: the triangle inequality.} With
$r = (\omega_{\rm nom} - \hat\omega) + e_\omega + n$ and (P.5)--(P.6),
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  |r_i| \;\le\; |e_{\omega,i}| \;+\; \sqrt{P_{ii}}\,\|e_\omega\|
  \;+\; |n_i| \;+\; \sqrt{P_{ii}}\,\|n\| \;}
  \tag{P.7}
\end{equation}
Every term is $e_\omega$ or $n$. The deviation enters twice, once
pointwise and once in norm, and the second copy is the displacement of
the fit --- which is exactly what (P.2) was missing and why it fails at
the onset, where $|e_\omega(\tau)|$ vanishes and $\|e_\omega\|$ does
not.

\paragraph{The noise term, sharpened.} Bounding $\|Pn\|$ by $\|n\|$
throws away the only thing that makes a projector harmless. For
$n\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_n^2I)$ and $P$ symmetric idempotent,
$\mathbb E\|Pn\|^2 = \sigma_n^2\operatorname{tr}P = 2\sigma_n^2$
regardless of $N$, and
$\operatorname{Var}[(Pn)_i] = \sigma_n^2P_{ii}$, so the last two terms
of (P.7) are together at most $k\sigma_n(1+\sqrt{P_{ii}})$ at
confidence $2\Phi(k)-1$, against the $\sigma_n\sqrt N$ that $\|n\|$
would give --- a factor of $4.5$ at $N = 41$. The form used below is
therefore
\begin{equation}
  |r_i| \;\le\; \underbrace{E_i}_{\text{pointwise}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\sqrt{P_{ii}}\,\|E\|}_{\text{displacement}}
  \;+\; k\sigma_n\bigl(1+\sqrt{P_{ii}}\bigr),
  \tag{P.8}
\end{equation}
using $|e_{\omega,i}|\le E_i$ and $\|e_\omega\|\le\|E\|$ from (93).
Two Gaussian events enter per sample, so a union bound over the window
replaces $k$ by $\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/4N)$: for $N = 53$ that is $3.50$ at
$95\%$ and $3.90$ at $99\%$, against $1.96$ and $2.58$ for one sample.

\subsection{Why this route and not the band}

The displacement can also be bounded by carrying the band into the
projector's kernel, $|(Pe_\omega)_i| \le \sum_j|P_{ij}|E_j$, which is
sharp over the box $\{|e_{\omega,j}|\le E_j\}$. That was the earlier
form of this section and it is worth saying what replacing it buys.

\emph{Weaker hypotheses.} The box argument needs the band pointwise at
every sample. (P.7) needs $\|e_\omega\|\le\|E\|$, one number, and
otherwise only what (VIII.3) already assumes: $\omega_{\rm nom}$ in the
family, and a global minimum.

\emph{It survives freeing $C_2$.} Step 1 in the form (P.3) is
projection-free, so with $C_2$ fitted --- as the calibration stage does
--- the displacement is still bounded, by $2\|e_\omega+n\|$. Only step 2
needs replacing, by the corresponding inequality on the two-dimensional
manifold. The curvature (reach) correction that the box route needed
throughout is confined to that one step.

\emph{It measures tighter.} Applied to every sample of all $140$ runs
the box form leaves a worst ratio of $0.763$ and (P.8) leaves $0.611$,
because $\sqrt{P_{ii}}$ falls near the onset while $\sum_j|P_{ij}|E_j$
does not.

The trace is $2$ at every rate and every window length, being the
dimension of the range, and that is why the constants do not grow with
the record: a two-dimensional projector cannot move much, however many
samples it is given. The displacement term is $1.09$ to $1.29$ times
$\sup E$ across all $140$ runs, so in uniform form
\begin{equation*}
  \max_i|r_i| \;\lesssim\; 2.3\,\sup_\tau E(\tau)
  \;+\; 1.4\,k\sigma_n .
\end{equation*}

$P$ itself is the standard least-squares projector: minimising
$\|y-A\theta\|^2$ gives the normal equations $A^{\!\top}\!A\hat\theta =
A^{\!\top}y$, whose content is $A^{\!\top}(y-A\hat\theta) = 0$ --- the
residual is orthogonal to everything the model can produce. For these
two columns it collapses to a form with no matrix inverse left in it,
\begin{equation}
  P_{ij} \;=\; \frac1N
   \;+\; \frac{\tilde u_i\,\tilde u_j}{\sum_k\tilde u_k^{\,2}},
  \qquad \tilde u = u - \bar u ,
  \tag{P.9}
\end{equation}
the constant channel plus the $u$ channel with the constant removed,
reproducing the numerically formed $P$ to $10^{-16}$. Every property
used above is one line from it: $P^2 = P$ and $P^{\!\top} = P$ are
immediate, $Pv = v$ on $V$ gives (P.5), $\operatorname{Var}[(Pn)_i] =
\sigma_n^2P_{ii}$ gives the noise term, and
$\operatorname{tr}P = 1 + 1 = 2$. It also locates the worst sample:
$P_{ii}$ is largest where $|\tilde u_i|$ is, which is the last sample of
the window in all $140$ runs, and only $1/N = 0.013$ to $0.024$ of
$P_{ii}$ there is the constant --- the rest is the amplitude direction,
whose $\cosh$ has grown by then.

The bound holds on all $140$ runs at every sample, the worst reaching
$0.611$ of it. Two conditions on how it is applied are not optional, and
the table makes both visible.

\paragraph{The noise scale must be measured in the window.} Taking
$\sigma_n$ from the pre-onset record leaves $123$ of $140$ inside, and
the shortfall is at the fast end. That is not the theorem failing but
its hypothesis: before the onset the vehicle is carried by every landing
gear, after it pivots on one edge, and the in-window disturbance is
$0.71$ to $0.86^\circ$/s against a pre-onset floor of $0.04$ to $0.17$.
The honest estimate is the in-window content above $5$~Hz, which the
fitted model provably cannot produce --- $\hat\omega$ is smooth on the
scale of the window --- so whatever is there is disturbance by
construction rather than by assumption.

\paragraph{A pinned amplitude adds a term.} $V$ is the family the bound
is about: amplitude and level free. The shipped estimator frees neither.
It takes $C_1 = K\Mdot$ from the calibration stage, and it forms the
level $C$ from the \emph{pre-onset} segment rather than minimising over
it on the window, so once the onset is chosen its post-onset curve has
no free parameter at all and $\hat\omega\ne Py$. Step 1 then removes
only part of the nominal and two terms survive,
\begin{equation}
  |r_i| \;\le\; |\Delta C_1|\,u_i + |C-b|
        + E_i + \sqrt{P_{ii}}\,\|E\|
        + k\sigma_n\bigl(1+\sqrt{P_{ii}}\bigr),
  \tag{P.8$'$}
\end{equation}
the first being the calibration's amplitude error and the second the
baseline channel of (105)--(106). Their measured sizes are very
different. The amplitude term is $|\Delta C_1|/C_1 = 11.3\%$ and it is
what puts $26$ runs outside the RMS form (VIII.3); without it the
deployed residual reaches $1.21$ of the bound. The baseline term is
$0.005$ to $0.029^\circ$/s, under $2\%$ of $\sup E$. One constraint
matters and the other does not, and the bound says which.

\subsection{What the calibration is worth: step 1 needs $C_2$ exact}
\label{sec:c2}

Step 1 removes the nominal because it lies in the family, and the family
is built from the calibrated $C_2$. If that value is not the true one
the nominal leaves $V$ and the cancellation is only partial: with
$C_2 = C_2^{*}(1+\varepsilon_2)$ the residual retains
$(I-P)\,\omega_{\rm nom}$, and (P.8) gains a further term. This is the
price of conditioning on the calibration stage --- which fits $C_2$
freely, by a per-run nonlinear least squares and a per-configuration
median --- and it is small, almost linear in $\varepsilon_2$:

A $2\%$ exponent error costs $1$ to $4\%$ of the envelope and a $5\%$
error costs $3$ to $11\%$, the fast ramp being the more exposed because
its window is shorter and the two hyperbolic shapes are less separated
there. Like the pinned amplitude it enters additively and is not
covered by $\rbar$, which bounds the physics the model omits and says
nothing about the constants the model is given.

The empirical check above is unaffected by any of this: it compares a
\emph{measured} record, which contains whatever exponent the rig
actually has, against the envelope, so the $140/140$ already includes
the misspecification. What the term supplies is the proof that this had
to be so, and a budget line for it.
