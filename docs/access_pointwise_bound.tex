\subsection{What is to be proved, and what is not true}

Write the recorded rate over the post-onset window as the nominal
response, the deviation the model does not carry, and sensor noise,
\begin{equation}
  y(\tau) = \omega_{\rm nom}(\tau) + e_\omega(\tau) + n(\tau),
  \tag{P.1}
\end{equation}
and let $\hat\omega$ be the fitted curve. The natural statement to want
about the residual $r = y - \hat\omega$ is
\begin{equation}
  |r(\tau)| \;\overset{?}{\le}\; |e_\omega(\tau)| + k\sigma_n .
  \tag{P.2}
\end{equation}
It is false, and the counterexample is at the onset. The envelope of
(93) vanishes there, $E(\tau)\propto\sinh C_2\tau\to0$, and so does
$e_\omega$; the residual does not. The fitted curve is selected by the
whole window, so a deviation living at large $\tau$ displaces the fit at
$\tau = 0$ as well, and (P.2) would require that displacement to vanish
where $e_\omega$ does. Measured on the campaign, the ratio
$\bigl(E+D\bigr)/E$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:thm} below is $2.07$ to $2.15$ at
the end of the window and $27$ to $95$ at the first sample: the
multiplicative form has no uniform constant.

What is missing from (P.2) is the displacement of the fit itself. Naming
it costs one term and makes the statement provable.

\subsection{The residual is exactly a projection}
\label{sec:thm}

The identification runs in two stages and they must not be confused
here. Stage one estimates $(C_2,K)$ for a configuration by a
\emph{free} per-run nonlinear least squares followed by a
per-configuration median, refined on a ramp-invariance criterion; that
fit is nonlinear and this section says nothing about it. Stage two --- the
per-run onset fit whose residual Sec.~VIII checks --- takes those two as
\emph{given constants}. Everything below is a statement about stage two,
conditional on the calibration it is handed, and Sec.~\ref{sec:c2} pays
for that conditioning.

With $C_2$ a constant the model is \emph{linear} in the amplitude and
the pre-onset level, so on the sample grid $\tau_1,\dots,\tau_N$ of the
window the admissible curves form a genuine two-dimensional subspace
\begin{equation}
  V = \operatorname{span}\{u,\mathbf 1\}
    = \operatorname{range} A,
  \quad
  A = \bigl[\,u \;\; \mathbf 1\,\bigr]\in\reals^{N\times2},
  \tag{P.3}
\end{equation}
with $u_i = \cosh C_2\tau_i - 1$. This is the one structural fact the
whole argument needs, and it is worth stating explicitly because the
estimator is elsewhere described as a nonlinear fit: it is nonlinear in
$C_2$, which stage one estimates and stage two does not touch, and in
the onset, which is swept rather than minimised over analytically. In
the parameters that remain it is linear.

Let $P = A(A^{\!\top}\!A)^{-1}A^{\!\top}$ be the orthogonal projector
onto $V$, so that the least-squares fit is $\hat\omega = Py$.

The constant $\mathbf 1$ belongs in the span because the fitted curve
carries an additive pre-onset level: (98) minimises over both segments
with the same offset $C$, and the post-onset model is
$\hat\omega(t;t_{\rm c})+C$. That level is \emph{not} gyro bias --- the rate
comes from the navigation filter, whose delta-angle bias states remove
sensor bias --- but the residual motion the vehicle still has when the
excitation begins, measured here at $0.004$ to $0.047^\circ$/s. What
$V$ may contain is dictated by what the estimator ranges over and not
chosen for convenience: writing $V = \operatorname{span}\{u\}$ while the
code also fits a level would make $\hat\omega\ne Py$ and Lemma~1 false.

The second dimension is close to free. Dropping $\mathbf 1$ would take
$\operatorname{tr}P$ from $2$ to $1$ and the constant of (P.7) from
$2.07$--$2.15$ down to $2.04$--$2.12$, a gain of $1.2\%$; keeping it
annihilates the constant channel entirely, and a constant is a
direction $u$ cannot mimic at all --- $u$ vanishes at the onset, so a
level $b$ would survive into the residual with a factor of $1.000$.

\paragraph{Lemma 1 (the nominal cancels).} $\omega_{\rm nom}\in V$,
being $C_1^{*}u$ for the true amplitude, so $P\omega_{\rm nom} =
\omega_{\rm nom}$ and
\begin{equation}
  r = (I-P)\,y = (I-P)\bigl(e_\omega + n\bigr) .
  \tag{P.4}
\end{equation}
The nominal response has disappeared entirely: the residual is a
function of the perturbations alone, exactly and with no approximation.
This is the step at which the intuition behind (P.2) becomes an
identity, and everything after it is bookkeeping on a
two-dimensional projector.

\paragraph{Lemma 2 (the deviation channel).} By (93) the deviation is
confined to the band $|e_{\omega,j}|\le E_j$. A linear functional over a
box attains its extremum at a vertex, so for each $i$
\begin{equation}
  \bigl|(Pe_\omega)_i\bigr| \;\le\; D_i
  \;\triangleq\; \sum_{j}\bigl|P_{ij}\bigr|E_j ,
  \tag{P.5}
\end{equation}
with equality for $e_{\omega,j} = E_j\operatorname{sgn}P_{ij}$. The
bound is sharp over the band, though conservative over the deviations
that are actually reachable, those being Duhamel images of an admissible
$\rho$ and therefore smooth.

\paragraph{Lemma 3 (the noise channel).} For $n\sim\mathcal N(0,
\sigma_n^2 I)$ and $P$ symmetric idempotent,
$\operatorname{Var}\bigl[(Pn)_i\bigr] = \sigma_n^2(P^2)_{ii} =
\sigma_n^2 P_{ii}$, so $|(Pn)_i|\le k\sigma_n\sqrt{P_{ii}}$ at
confidence $2\Phi(k)-1$.

\paragraph{Theorem.} Combining (P.4) with the two lemmas,
\begin{equation}
  \bigl|r(\tau_i)\bigr| \;\le\;
  \underbrace{E_i}_{\text{model}} +
  \underbrace{D_i}_{\substack{\text{displacement}\\\text{of the fit}}} +
  \; k\sigma_n\bigl(1 + \sqrt{P_{ii}}\bigr) ,
  \tag{P.6}
\end{equation}
and, writing $\Lambda_E = \max_i D_i/\max_j E_j$ and $\kappa =
\max_i\sqrt{P_{ii}}$, in uniform form
\begin{equation}
  \max_i|r_i| \;\le\;
  \bigl(1+\Lambda_E\bigr)\sup_\tau E(\tau)
  \;+\;\bigl(1+\kappa\bigr)k\sigma_n .
  \tag{P.7}
\end{equation}
Two Gaussian events enter per sample, $n_i$ and $(Pn)_i$, so a union
bound over the window replaces $k$ by $\Phi^{-1}\!\bigl(1-\alpha/4N\bigr)$:
for $N = 53$ samples that is $k = 3.50$ at $95\%$ and $3.90$ at $99\%$,
against $1.96$ and $2.58$ for a single sample.

\subsection{The constants, and why they do not grow}

Everything in (P.6) is computable before a run is flown: $P$ depends
only on $C_2$ and the sample grid, $E$ only on the geometry box.

$P$ itself is the standard least-squares projector. Minimising
$\|y-A\theta\|^2$ gives the normal equations
$A^{\!\top}\!A\hat\theta = A^{\!\top}y$, whose content is
$A^{\!\top}(y - A\hat\theta) = 0$: the residual is orthogonal to every
column of $A$, which is to say to everything the model can produce.
With $A$ of full column rank, $\hat\theta = (A^{\!\top}\!A)^{-1}
A^{\!\top}y$ and $\hat\omega = A\hat\theta = Py$. For the two columns
here it collapses to a form with no matrix inverse left in it,
\begin{equation}
  P_{ij} \;=\; \frac1N
   \;+\; \frac{\tilde u_i\,\tilde u_j}{\sum_k \tilde u_k^{\,2}},
  \qquad
  \tilde u = u - \bar u ,
  \tag{P.9}
\end{equation}
which is the constant channel plus the $u$ channel with the constant
removed, and it reproduces the numerically formed $P$ to $10^{-16}$.
Every property the proof uses is one line from it. $P^2 = P$ and
$P^{\!\top} = P$ are immediate; $Pv = v$ for $v\in V$ is Lemma~1;
$\operatorname{Var}[(Pn)_i] = \sigma_n^2(P P^{\!\top})_{ii} =
\sigma_n^2P_{ii}$ is Lemma~3; and
\begin{equation*}
  \operatorname{tr}P
  = \sum_i\frac1N + \frac{\sum_i\tilde u_i^{\,2}}{\sum_k\tilde u_k^{\,2}}
  = 1 + 1 = 2 ,
\end{equation*}
one unit for each basis direction, whatever $N$ is. Equation (P.9) also
locates the worst sample: $P_{ii}$ is largest where $|\tilde u_i|$ is,
which is the last sample of the window, $\tau/\tau_{\rm end} = 1.000$ in
every one of the $140$ runs. There $P_{ii}$ reaches $0.124$ to $0.164$,
of which only $1/N = 0.013$ to $0.024$ is the constant --- the rest is
the amplitude direction, whose $\cosh$ has grown by then. This is why
$\kappa$ is $0.35$ to $0.40$ rather than the $\sqrt{2/N}\approx0.2$ a
flat basis would give.

The trace is $2$ at every rate and every window length, being the
dimension of the range, and that is the reason the constants do not grow
with the record: a two-dimensional projector cannot move much, however
many samples it is given. The Lebesgue constant $\Lambda = \max_i\sum_j
|P_{ij}|$, which is what (P.5) reduces to if $E$ is replaced by its
supremum, sits between $1.92$ and $2.10$ across a twelvefold change in
ramp rate. Carrying the actual shape of $E$ instead gives
$1+\Lambda_E\simeq2.1$, about a third smaller. Over all $140$ runs
\begin{equation*}
  \max|r| \;\le\; 2.18\,\sup E \;+\; 1.48\,k\sigma_n .
\end{equation*}

The theorem holds on all $140$ runs at every sample, the worst ratio
being $0.763$. Two conditions on how it is applied are not optional, and
the table makes both visible.

\paragraph{The noise scale must be measured in the window.} Taking
$\sigma_n$ from the pre-onset record leaves only $74$ of $140$ runs
inside. That is not the theorem failing but its hypothesis: before the
onset the vehicle is carried by every landing gear, after it pivots on
one edge, and the in-window disturbance is $0.71$ to $0.86^\circ$/s
against a pre-onset floor of $0.04$ to $0.17$. The honest estimate is
the in-window content above $5$~Hz, which the fitted model provably
cannot produce --- $\hat\omega$ is smooth on the scale of the window ---
so whatever is there is disturbance by construction rather than by
assumption.

\paragraph{The deployed fit is more constrained, and each constraint
adds a term.} $V$ is the family the bound is about: amplitude and level
free. The shipped estimator frees neither. It takes $C_1 = K\Mdot$ from
the calibration stage, and it forms $C$ from the \emph{pre-onset}
segment rather than minimising over it on the window, so once the onset
is chosen its post-onset curve has no free parameter at all and
$\hat\omega\ne Py$. Lemma~1 then removes only part of the nominal and
two terms survive,
\begin{equation}
  |r_i| \;\le\; |\Delta C_1|\,u_i + |C-b|
        + E_i + D_i + k\sigma_n\bigl(1+\sqrt{P_{ii}}\bigr),
  \tag{P.6$'$}
\end{equation}
the first being the calibration's amplitude error and the second the
baseline channel already carried through (105)--(106). Their measured
sizes are very different. The amplitude term is $|\Delta C_1|/C_1 =
11.3\%$ and it is what puts $26$ runs outside the RMS form (VIII.3);
without it the deployed residual reaches $1.52$ of the bound. The
baseline term is $|C-b|\simeq\sigma_{\rm pre}/\sqrt{N_{\rm pre}} =
0.005$ to $0.029^\circ$/s, which is $0.1$ to $1.8\%$ of $\sup E$
($8.6\%$ at worst over the $140$ runs). One constraint matters and the
other does not, and the bound says which.

\subsection{What the calibration is worth: Lemma 1 needs $C_2$ exact}
\label{sec:c2}

Lemma~1 removes the nominal because $\omega_{\rm nom}\in V$, and $V$ is
built from the calibrated $C_2$. If that value is not the true one the
nominal leaves the subspace and the cancellation is only partial: with
$C_2 = C_2^{*}(1+\varepsilon_2)$ the residual retains
$(I-P)\,\omega_{\rm nom}$, and (P.6) gains a fourth term
\begin{equation}
  |r_i| \;\le\; \bigl|\bigl[(I-P)\omega_{\rm nom}\bigr]_i\bigr|
   + |\Delta C_1|u_i + E_i + D_i
   + k\sigma_n\bigl(1+\sqrt{P_{ii}}\bigr).
  \tag{P.8}
\end{equation}
This is the price of conditioning on stage one, and it is the reason the
two stages had to be separated at the start. It is small, and almost
linear in $\varepsilon_2$:

A $2\%$ exponent error costs $1$ to $4\%$ of the envelope and a $5\%$
error costs $3$ to $11\%$, the fast ramp being the more exposed because
its window is shorter and the two hyperbolic shapes are less separated
there. Like the pinned amplitude it enters additively and is not
covered by $\rbar$, which bounds the physics the model omits and says
nothing about the constants the model is given.

Note that the empirical check above is unaffected by any of this: it
compares a \emph{measured} record, which contains whatever exponent the
rig actually has, against the envelope, so the $140/140$ already
includes the misspecification. What (P.8) supplies is the proof that
this had to be so, and a budget line for it.

\paragraph{If $C_2$ is freed, as in stage one.} $V$ is then a
two-dimensional manifold rather than a subspace and $P$ is no longer a
projector, so none of the above applies verbatim. The statement survives
with a curvature factor: if the manifold has reach $\varrho$ and
$\|e_\omega+n\| < \varrho$, the displacement in (P.5) is multiplied by
$\bigl(1-\|e_\omega+n\|/\varrho\bigr)^{-1}$. That is the form to use for
the calibration fit itself. For the per-run fit that Sec.~VIII checks,
$C_2$ is a constant and the linear form applies exactly, which is worth
saying plainly: the projection argument is exact for the stage the
residual test is about, not an approximation to it.
